# Rooted: Product Brief v0.3

**Course:** New Venture Strategy, Chicago Booth | **Status:** Draft for team review | **Date:** Nov 9, 2026

> **Evidence labels.** **[Team]** = team decision, **[Research]** = external source a teammate must open and verify, **[Assumption]** = AI inference to test in interviews. Nothing here is customer evidence yet.

## 1. One-line summary

**Rooted is Strava for staying close to your kid:** an app that tells busy parents what to ask their middle schooler, and when, then turns those conversations into a habit that gets smarter as more parents join. **[Team]**

## 2. Name, vision and mission

- **Working name:** Rooted **[Team]** (check App Store, domains, USPTO before locking in)
- **Vision:** Every parent stays close to their kid through the teenage years, no matter how busy life gets.
- **Mission:** Turn the small moments busy parents already have (car rides, dinner, bedtime) into real conversations.

## 3. The problem

Teens stop opening up, and parents have no good way to get them talking again. Around 11 to 14 kids turn toward friends and share less at home **[Research: verify]**; busy parents default to “How was school?” → “Fine.” **[Assumption]**; parents react by asking or monitoring more, teens share less **[Assumption]**; most of what parents know comes from the kid choosing to share (Stattin and Kerr, 2000) **[Research: verify]**.

## 4. Key insight and positioning

**Everyone built tools to watch kids. Nobody built tools to help parents connect with them.** Hinge (a specific prompt gets a real answer), We’re Not Really Strangers (start light, go deeper), Strava (tracking your own effort plus community), Habitica (streaks and rewards), Life360/Bark (monitoring answers “where”, not “how”).

**Positioning:** “Zero data from kids. All the help goes to parents.”

## 5. Target customer

User = buyer = payer: the parent. Kids never use the app. **[Team]** Beachhead: parents of 6th graders in top-performing Boston-area districts; long term all parents of grades 6 to 12, including lower-income families through employers and schools.

## 6. Personas

**Dana** (44, dual-career, 7th grader): “I used to know everything about her day. Now I get one-word answers.” **Marcus** (38, shift worker, single parent of a 6th grader): reached through an employer. **Anti-persona:** parents whose goal is monitoring; families in crisis who need a therapist.

## 7. Job to be done

“When I have a few minutes with my kid and don’t know what to say, help me ask something that gets a real answer, so I stay close to what’s happening in their life.” Triggers: pickup, car ride, dinner, a big event coming up.

## 8. Product principles

1. Parent-only. 2. Under 10 seconds. 3. The parent’s own voice. 4. Coach, don’t monitor. 5. Get better for this family. 6. Share effort, never content. 7. Encourage, don’t rank.

## 9. Feature set

| # | Feature | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kid profile (grade, interests, activities) | MVP |
| 2 | Schedule input (academics + extracurriculars) | MVP |
| 3 | AI questions based on the schedule (3 per event) | MVP |
| 4 | Parent chooses + asks in person | MVP |
| 5 | Conversation rating (1 to 10) | MVP |
| 6 | Feedback on every prompt (worked / didn’t) | MVP |
| 7 | Weekly Sunday check-in | MVP |
| 8–15 | Smart timing, response tips, notes, learning AI, levels, growing tree, streaks/badges, community prompts | MLP |
| 16–20 | Parent groups, calendar sync, topic packs, co-parent sharing, employer/school edition | V2 |

**Not building:** a teen app, location tracking, message monitoring, recordings, an AI therapist, leaderboards, sharing what a kid said.

## 10. MVP: does the core loop work?

Sunday check-in → 3 questions per event during the week → one tap after each conversation → prompts improve. Success (4-week pilot, ~30 Boston parents): 2+ questions used per week; average rating rises; 50%+ active in week 4.

## 11–20

MLP success: 30-day retention 40%+, 5%+ upgrade, “very disappointed” in surveys. Network effects: ratings data and school groups. Roadmap Q1–Q4 2027: validate, make lovable, monetize in Boston, expand. Freemium at $9.99/month or ~$79/year. GTM through parent associations, youth sports, group chats. Metrics: north star = share of conversations rated 7+. Risks: habit, willingness to pay, gamification guilt, scripted feel, privacy of notes/groups, AI safety (human-reviewed library), copycats. Open questions: name availability, beachhead, price, schedule entry method, 1–10 vs 3-option tap, social layer, strategy model.
